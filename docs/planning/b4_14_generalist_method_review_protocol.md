## Independent mechanism derivation and next-slice boundary

For a future network prediction `z`, the candidate physical objective is
`C(F(P(z))) / C_train`, where `P` is TopoLab's filtered-volume projection,
`F` its existing density filter, and `C_train` an offline training-label
normalizer. The existing SIMP adjoint supplies the derivative at the **current
prediction**, unlike fixed target-sensitivity-weighted density MSE. Deriving
the gradient through projection, bounds/active sets and filter is required;
a stop-gradient approximation must not silently claim that derivative.
No such loss or derivative is implemented or evaluated in B4.14.

The prospective B4.15 must separately freeze eight train-only cases (two
canonical cases per mesh/direction cell, before label access), directional
finite-difference and physical-volume checks, full forward/backward timing,
memory and extrapolated bounded-fit/amortization costs. It fits no network,
generates no new uniform label and opens no fresh/final outcome. A finite
compute contract and a negative-result stop rule precede the probe. Only a
passing feasibility result could justify registering a later fixed-weight loss,
three-seed fit and fresh repair screen. Existing P/17 remains the fixed control;
no seed/checkpoint is replaced here. A new fit must preserve all controls and
be independently versioned before a separate repair and confirmation.

The proposed loss adds no **new query-time physics input solve by design**;
future inference, projection, refinement and fallback costs remain measured.
Offline FEM/adjoint work can be expensive and must be charged, profiled and
amortized. Initial compliance is not a terminal-quality or basin certificate;
even correct gradients do not guarantee generalization or less refinement.
